Verify, Act, Distill: Turning Test-Time Compute into Test-Time Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models

🗓️ 2026-10-10 ✍️ Kaneyoshi Hiratsuka, Satoshi Yamamori, Jun Morimoto
#reinforcement learning#vision-language-action#vla

Test-time compute (TTC), which scores multiple candidate actions at inference time, is a powerful way to improve the execution performance of vision-language-action (VLA) models, but existing methods keep the policy frozen, so the gain must be recomputed at every execution. We propose Verify, Act, Distill, a framework that consolidates the gains of TTC into the VLA itself. A fixed verifier scores candidates generated by a flow-matching VLA, the weighted aggregate is executed, and the observations and aggregated chunks from successful adaptation episodes are distilled directly into LoRA parameters with the standard flow-matching loss. This reuses supervision that TTC has already produced during execution, without additional reinforcement learning or rollouts dedicated to teacher generation. On the 40 LIBERO tasks with a SmolVLA 5k checkpoint, distilling from only 10 episodes per task raises the mean success rate of verifier-free single-candidate generation from 54.1% to 70.6%, retaining about 74% of the improvement obtained by aggregating eight candidates. Applying the same aggregation to the distilled policy further raises the success rate from 76.4% to 82.3%, with a particularly large gain on LIBERO-Long (44.0% to 57.8%), and the single-candidate policy also improves on LIBERO-plus, which contains distribution shifts. These results show that using verification as a learning signal, rather than only as an execution-time selector, both retains the improvements of TTC and re-amplifies the effect of subsequent TTC.

CFG-Policy: Unified Diffusion Policy Learning Framework via Classifier-free Guidance

🗓️ 2026-10-10 ✍️ Shutong Ding, Zhaoyang Liu, Yimiao Zhou et al.
#reinforcement learning#diffusion policy#vision-language-action

Diffusion policies are increasingly used in robotic control and manipulation, vision-language-action models, and world-model-based agents. Their ability to represent multimodal action distributions offers rich opportunities for exploration in reinforcement learning (RL). However, optimizing diffusion policies with RL can reduce the behavioral diversity and further limit their exploration capability. In RL post-training, directly fine-tuning a pretrained policy may also disrupt its acquired capabilities, while action-space residuals may lack the expressive power for multimodal exploration. Together, these issues reveal a common challenge across online and offline-to-online diffusion RL: improving task performance while retaining the behavioral diversity and capabilities needed for continued adaptation. We introduce CFG-Policy, a unified, plug-and-play framework that separates exploration and exploitation into two generative policies and combines their score or velocity fields during sampling through classifier-free guidance. For online RL, a frozen exploration branch sustains behavioral diversity while an exploitation branch learns from task rewards; for offline-to-online RL, a pretrained exploitation branch remains frozen while a trainable branch steers the composed behavior. CFG-Policy preserves the underlying RL objectives and improves performance with QSM and QVPO on 5 MuJoCo tasks and with Flow-SDE+PPO on 4 RoboTwin manipulation tasks. Project page is at https://zhy-liu.github.io/cfg-policy-page.

DEFT-VLA: Stage-Localized Force Specialization for Contact-Rich Robotic Manipulation

🗓️ 2026-10-10 ✍️ Zan Huang, Yibo Qiu, Kan Ding et al.
#vision-language-action#vla

Contact-rich manipulation poses a stage-dependent sensory conflict: vision and proprioception are essential for localization and approach, when force is largely uninformative and potentially distracting, whereas force becomes critical after contact, when irrelevant visual details and correlated robot states may become shortcuts. We propose DEFT-VLA (Dual-Expert Force-specialized Training for Vision-Language-Action Manipulation), which retains the task-adapted base expert for vision-dominated non-contact behavior and introduces a force-aware contact expert for critical contact stages, localizing force-conditioned adaptation to where it is needed. We further introduce a contact-localized force-attending curriculum that applies force-attending post-training only to the contact expert, progressively restoring vision and masking state tokens to suppress shortcuts. Real-robot experiments on pipette tip attachment, bimanual pipette aspiration, and whiteboard wiping show that DEFT-VLA effectively exploits force feedback to improve contact-stage performance while maintaining strong non-contact behavior and robustness to held-out grasp heights. It improves overall task success by 20-40 percentage points over the strongest baseline on each task.

Push the Limit: Learning High-Speed Sprinting and Aggressive Cornering on a Full-Scale Humanoid

🗓️ 2026-10-10 ✍️ Huaxing Huang, Ruidong Li, Jinchao Han et al.
#humanoid#bipedal

We present a learning-based framework that enables a full-scale bipedal humanoid to sprint at over 7 m/s with motion-prior-guided gait and perform aggressive cornering at high speed with a pronounced trunk lean of up to $25^\circ$. Achieving this combination is challenging: above approximately 4 m/s, periodic foot impacts induce severe, high-frequency yaw-rate oscillations intrinsic to high-speed gaits. This high-frequency temporal structure corrupts the history of proprioceptive feedback that flat-MLP policies rely on, while rendering conventional yaw-rate tracking rewards counterproductive, as they fight against the robot's natural oscillatory dynamics. Moreover, a single style prior shared across skills cannot tell which skill is commanded and inherently compromises the low-speed skill fidelity. To overcome these limitations, we adopt a causal multi-head attention (MHA) module to explicitly model the high-frequency temporal feedback. In place of the oscillatory yaw rate, we design a heading-direction reward that supervises only the low-frequency velocity direction, allowing agile turning at high speed without suppressing natural gait oscillations. We further introduce skill-conditioned multi-adversarial-motion-prior (MAMP) discriminators that condition the style reward on the commanded skill. Ablation experiments confirm that each mechanism is essential: replacing structured temporal encoding with a flat-history MLP caps the top speed; reverting to yaw-rate tracking prevents high-speed turning; and a single shared discriminator makes in-place turning collapse into a quasi-static stance. Our framework bridges the gap between raw sprinting speed and reference-motion fidelity, demonstrating stable high-speed sprinting and aggressive cornering in both simulation and real-robot deployment.

Test-Time Compute for Humanoid Behavior Foundation Models

🗓️ 2026-10-10 ✍️ Weiji Xie, Junsong Wu, Zibo Zhou et al.
#humanoid#teleoperation

Humanoid Behavior Foundation Models (BFMs) can execute impressive motions, but downstream applications such as loco-manipulation demand high whole-body positional accuracy. Existing BFMs suffer from a systematic command-execution gap, where executed motions deviate from desired ones. We show that additional test-time compute can improve execution without retraining the BFM. First, we introduce Test-Time Command Optimization (TTCO), which adapts the input command to the specific BFM to produce an executed trajectory closer to the desired motion. TTCO finds such commands by sampling-based optimization through closed-loop simulations of the BFM and robot dynamics. Across three BFMs on the AMASS dataset, TTCO reduces mean global body-position error by 79-84% in simulation. Second, we analyze test-time compute scaling and derive an approximate compute-optimal strategy that can sustain performance gains as the compute budget increases. Third, TTCO can generate BFM-specific action labels from recorded robot trajectories without requiring the original teleoperation body-motion commands. Simulation and real-robot experiments show that these labels are effective for training high-level loco-manipulation policies. This method also supports reusing existing demonstrations across BFMs.

Beyond Fixed Visual History: Learning Motion-Adaptive Temporal Receptive Fields for Complex-Terrain Quadruped Locomotion

🗓️ 2026-10-10 ✍️ Hao Zhang, Yuping Yan, Yitian Hong et al.
#quadruped#locomotion

Versatile quadruped locomotion depends on a tight coupling between what the robot perceives and what its body can execute. Yet existing visual controllers typically organize depth history using fixed temporal patterns, overlooking that the same temporal span corresponds to different spatial ranges as the robot's speed, posture, and gait phase change. Consequently, the same visual history may be insufficient in one motion state but outdated or misaligned in another. To address this limitation, we propose TempoLoco, a body-aware dynamic visual perception framework that organizes visual memory according to the robot's current locomotion state. TempoLoco learns multiple complementary temporal views from a long depth buffer, encodes them within a shared feature space, and dynamically aggregates them using proprioceptive history and multi-scale terrain evidence. This enables a single policy to align visual anticipation with the robot's current motion capabilities across complex terrains, including stairs, platforms, gaps, discrete obstacles, rough ground, and slopes. From the experiment analysis, TempoLoco achieves a 78.4% success rate, compared with 58.2% for the strongest fixed-history baseline and 62.1% for uniform multi-scale fusion. In separate capability evaluations, it completes 41/45 simulated trials across nine terrain types and 23/25 real-world trials on a Unitree B2 with a fixed arm, demonstrating robust and deployable locomotion through body-aware dynamic visual perception.

Modeling and Control of a Rigid-soft Hybrid-link Humanoid Robot

🗓️ 2026-10-10 ✍️ Zewen He, Taiki Ishigaki, Ko Yamamoto
#humanoid

Physical flexibility plays a significant role not only in human motions but also in robot motions. For example, a humanoid robot with passive and flexible elements, which can be regarded as a rigid--soft hybrid-link system, has the advantages of storing energy and mitigating impact in a dynamic motion. However, the motion design and control of a hybrid-link system are challenging to achieve and have only been discussed in the literature. In this study, we propose the framework of inverse kinematics and compliance optimization-based control for a hybrid-link humanoid system that represents the continuous deformation of a flexible structure using the piece-wise constant strain (PCS) model. The proposed method was validated using forward dynamics simulations for standing, jumping, and walking motions. Then, we built an experimental setup using a biped humanoid robot with leaf-spring type prosthesis leg, in which the prosthesis deformation was measured by a motion capture system. Using this setup, we experimentally validated the proposed method for standing balance control in both double- and single-support cases.

Scalable Multi-Human Cooperative Manipulation via Commander-Executor Coordination

🗓️ 2026-10-10 ✍️ Buzhen Huang, Xingchen Wu, Yuanwang Yang et al.
#humanoid

Multi-human cooperative object manipulation is a highly challenging topic in humanoid control. While existing methods can handle simple collaborative carrying tasks, they struggle with role-dependent, long-horizon, and sequential cooperative manipulation. To address this issue, we propose a hierarchical commander-executor framework that enables multiple agents to accomplish complex collaborative tasks in distinct ways. Specifically, a commander combines instructions and scene information for joint semantic and geometric planning. It then decomposes the task into role-specific subtasks and generates shared object-centric coordination plans, specifying execution timing, target positions, and manipulation actions for each agent. Conditioned on the commander's high-level strategy and each agent's local observations, the executor learns a low-level physics-based policy through a solo-to-cooperative curriculum to realize coordinated human-object interactions. The planned object trajectory provides a shared coordination target, while root positions and grasping instructions specify role-dependent execution cues. We evaluate our method on a variety of object manipulation tasks, demonstrating its ability to execute long-horizon tasks that combine different types of collaboration and to scale from two-agent to multi-agent cooperation. Extensive experiments further show that our approach achieves higher success rates compared to state-of-the-art methods. Code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/TJU-3DV/Co-Manipulation.
